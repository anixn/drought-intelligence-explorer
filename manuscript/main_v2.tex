\section*{Abstract}
Drought records for India are usually long or multi-indicator or district-resolved, rarely all three, and seldom shipped with the code that produced them. We present version 2.0.0 of the Drought Intelligence Explorer, a monthly district-level resource for 724 districts in 36 States and Union Territories organised into three explicitly labelled eras that are never spliced. Era A is the Drought Atlas of India reconstruction, providing SPEI at 1-, 4- and 12-month scales for 1901--2025 on the same district fabric (3,220,872 district-month rows). Era B is the validated gridded multi-source product, extended here to September 2026: ten indicators derived from CHIRPS precipitation, ERA5-Land evapotranspiration and root-zone soil moisture, and MODIS vegetation, holding 3,780,163 valid district-month values. Era C contains bias-corrected CMIP6 simulations for 2015--2100 under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, with an eight-model ensemble, median and percentile bands, drought-threshold agreement counts and per-model members (19,904 Parquet partitions). An overlap product quantifies era-A--era-B agreement over 1981--2014, and a published-claim harness recomputes the statistics quoted in this paper. Validation is reported against independent data: a median district correlation of 0.604--0.714 against an independently processed IMD benchmark, 82.2--86.9\% drought-state agreement, median correlations of 0.717 (SPEI-1) and 0.667 (SPEI-12) between the reconstruction and the gridded record, and six committed CMIP6 release gates of which the two weather-reproduction diagnostics are reported but deliberately not gated. Missing values are never imputed. The complete archive is served from static files to a DuckDB-WASM engine in the browser, so every figure, table and export is re-derivable from public inputs without an account, a server or an API key.

\section*{Background \& Summary}
Drought is a slowly evolving hazard whose definition depends on the domain of impact. Meteorological, agricultural, hydrological and socio-economic drought respond to water deficits over different accumulation windows, and no single index represents all of them\citep{WilhiteGlantz1985,MishraSingh2010}. Standardized indices make these facets comparable. The Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) converts accumulated precipitation over a chosen window into a probability-based anomaly\citep{McKee1993}. The Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) applies the same machinery to precipitation minus potential evapotranspiration and therefore also responds to atmospheric evaporative demand\citep{VicenteSerrano2010}. Vegetation and soil-moisture indicators describe the land-surface response\citep{Kogan1995}. International guidance consequently recommends several indices at several time scales rather than a single drought number\citep{WMOLincoln2009,SvobodaFuchs2016}.

India is a demanding setting for such a record. Rainfall is concentrated in a strongly seasonal monsoon and varies sharply in space, while agriculture, water resources and ecosystems respond on different delays\citep{GadgilGadgil2006}. High-quality national gridded observation products exist\citep{Pai2014,Srivastava2009}, and several services publish drought information: the India Meteorological Department issues operational weekly assessments, the National Remote Sensing Centre publishes vegetation-based agricultural drought assessments, and the Drought Atlas of India supplies a high-resolution long-term SPEI reconstruction\citep{Chuphal2024,ChuphalData2024}. These products answer different questions. Operational services emphasise current conditions; the Atlas provides archive depth but one indicator family; and a researcher who needs several indicators at a stable district scale must otherwise assemble and document a new processing chain.

This paper describes version 2.0.0 of the Drought Intelligence Explorer (DIE), which integrates data, processing, validation and access in one citable contract. The record is organised into three eras (Fig.~1). \textbf{Era A} is the Atlas reconstruction for 1901--2025, containing SPEI-1, SPEI-4 and SPEI-12 regridded to the district fabric. \textbf{Era B} is a gridded multi-source product for January 1981--September 2026, containing SPI-1/3/6/12, SPEI-1/3/6/12, a root-zone soil-moisture anomaly (SMA) and the Vegetation Condition Index (VCI); its ten matrices contain 3,780,163 valid district-month values. \textbf{Era C} contains bias-corrected CMIP6 simulations for 2015--2100 under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5. The eras share district identifiers and a long-format schema, but retain separate source, reference-period and uncertainty labels and are never combined into an unqualified historical series.

Version 2.0.0 extends the published version 1.0.0 resource\citep{DeshmukhV1Data2026}. Era A, era C, the cross-validation products and the query engine are new to this version; era B preserves the published indicator semantics, event rules and monthly refresh policy while extending the record from December 2025 to September 2026. The contribution is therefore integration and validation rather than the invention of new drought indices. Public inputs include CHIRPS precipitation\citep{Funk2015}, ERA5-Land\citep{MunozSabater2021}, MODIS vegetation indices\citep{Didan2015}, the Drought Atlas\citep{ChuphalData2024} and CMIP6 simulations\citep{Eyring2016,ONeill2016}. Established SPI and SPEI definitions, quantile delta mapping\citep{Cannon2015} and Hargreaves--Samani potential evapotranspiration\citep{HargreavesSamani1985} are used throughout.

Three design commitments distinguish the resource. First, \emph{era honesty}: a reconstruction, a gridded multi-source product assembled from satellite, reanalysis and land-surface data, and a set of model simulations are different kinds of evidence, and the archive says so at the level of every row, figure and export. Second, \emph{no imputation}: missing observations remain missing, and every statistic reports the sample that produced it. Third, \emph{re-derivability}: the processing chain, the browser engine and the publication figures are all scripted, checksummed and archived, so that a reader can move from a displayed number to the exact Parquet partition and back.

The data support secondary analyses of drought frequency, duration, severity, seasonality, indicator relationships and model performance. They are not operational drought declarations. Projections are simulations rather than forecasts, reconstructions and reanalysis are not in-situ observations, and district aggregates do not acquire the native resolution of their source grids.

\section*{Study area and reporting fabric}
The reporting units are 724 unique district polygons across 36 States and Union Territories from the community \path{india-maps-data} repository\citep{uditIndiaMaps}. The source uses a 2011-era demarcation lineage and includes some units created after the 2011 census. The published fabric is deliberately fixed over the full record. Later district reorganisations are not crosswalked, because no validated spatio-temporal allocation is available and reallocating historical values across changing vintages would introduce an additional source of error.

All regional areas and area-weighted summaries use a Lambert Azimuthal Equal-Area (LAEA) projection centred at 22.5$^\circ$N, 79.5$^\circ$E. The served district fabric totals 3,266,386~km$^2$, approximately 0.64\% below India's published area of 3,287,263~km$^2$. The shortfall is primarily a property of the source geometry rather than of the web simplification: the unsimplified source fabric totals 3,266,465~km$^2$ for 726 features and 3,266,382~km$^2$ after removing two exact duplicates. Generalisation therefore accounts for only a few square kilometres. Median district-area error between the served and unsimplified geometries is 0.034\%, with a 95th percentile of 0.15\%. Boundaries are not authoritative for legal purposes and follow the depiction of the source dataset. The source repository does not declare an explicit redistribution licence, so boundary files are attributable, separable from the indicator matrices and accompanied by the source disclaimer; users requiring a formally licensed geometry can substitute another fabric with a documented crosswalk.

District values are area aggregates of gridded fields, not point measurements. Native resolution remains approximately 0.05$^\circ$ for CHIRPS and the Atlas, 0.1$^\circ$ for ERA5-Land and 1~km for MODIS. Small districts can depend on few source pixels, and neighbouring district estimates are not statistically independent.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Era-B extraction and aggregation}
For each month and variable, the Earth Engine route composites the native source grid, applies physical valid-range guards and reduces values over district polygons with \texttt{pixelArea()} weights. Daily CHIRPS precipitation is summed to monthly totals. ERA5-Land potential evaporation is sign-corrected and converted from metres to millimetres. The three ERA5-Land soil-water layers spanning 0--7, 7--28 and 28--100~cm are averaged with equal weight to form a 0--100~cm root-zone value; this documented simplification gives the shallow layer disproportionate weight, and a thickness-weighted alternative remains a sensitivity option rather than a change to the released record. MODIS granules are mosaicked before the NDVI scale factor is applied.

Extraction requests are divided into 12-month chunks, with recursive splitting and retry when memory limits are reached. Districts with no valid overlapping pixel receive a grid-matched buffer fallback: 6~km for CHIRPS, 16~km for ERA5-Land and 2~km for MODIS. The fallback is applied only when the unbuffered pixel count is zero, is recorded in the build log and changes the effective support of a small number of island units. Completed chunks are cached only when their month coverage matches exactly.

The indices are not calculated inside Earth Engine. SPI and SPEI require per-district, per-calendar-month distribution fits and inverse-normal transforms, which are small auditable Python operations. Keeping the statistical core outside the extraction vendor makes it testable offline and identical for the online and offline routes.

\subsection*{Indicators and standardization}
Ten indicators are calculated for each district and calendar month (Table~\ref{tab:indicators}). SPI uses a two-parameter gamma distribution fitted by maximum likelihood to positive precipitation totals, with a mixed distribution for zero-rain months\citep{McKee1993}. For district $i$, calendar month $m$ and accumulation window $k$, the non-exceedance probability of the accumulated precipitation is transformed to a standard normal deviate. Incomplete windows remain missing rather than being mapped through a floor probability.

SPEI applies the same transform to precipitation minus potential evapotranspiration. It uses a three-parameter log-logistic distribution fitted by L-moments\citep{VicenteSerrano2010,Begueria2014,Hosking1990}. Maximum likelihood is deliberately not used because the location parameter can collapse onto the sample minimum at operational sample sizes. Where a parametric fit fails its calibration checks or is incompatible with the sample, the entire district-calendar-month series is transformed with a tie-aware Gringorten plotting position\citep{Gringorten1963}. Candidate distributions for SPI and SPEI have been compared in previous evaluations\citep{Stagge2015}.

The SMA is the percentage departure of root-zone soil water from the same calendar month's climatological mean, and the VCI rescales district-mean NDVI between the historical minimum and maximum for the same calendar month\citep{Kogan1995}. NDVI is aggregated to districts before VCI is calculated, so the result is not an area-weighted mean of pixel-level VCI values.

SPI, SPEI and SMA use a fixed 1981--2020 reference period, giving 40 values per district-calendar-month fit. VCI uses February 2000 to the release date because the MODIS record is shorter. Values after the reference period are out-of-sample standardisations. A minimum of 20 base years is required, and transformed base values must have a mean within $\pm0.2$ of zero and a standard deviation between 0.75 and 1.3. A whole-series Gringorten fallback is used when a fit fails. Multi-month windows begin only when the full accumulation is available.

Era B has values for all 724 districts in SPI and VCI. SPEI and SMA have 723 because Lakshadweep has no valid ERA5-Land pixel. VCI has ten missing district-months within its 320-month window. Missing values are never imputed, interpolated or carried forward.

Class boundaries follow the nine-class McKee scheme: extreme drought at or below $-2.00$, severe $(-2.00,-1.50]$, moderate $(-1.50,-1.00]$, mild $(-1.00,-0.50]$ and near normal $(-0.50,0.50)$, with four wet classes above $+0.50$. SMA uses $-5/-15/-30/-50$\% thresholds, and VCI uses 0--10, 10--20, 20--30 and 30--40\% stress bands. These bounded-percentage thresholds are defined once in the metadata and are not replaced by z-score cut-offs.

\subsection*{Spatial aggregation and area statistics}
For a region $R$, the reported mean is $\bar{x}_R=\sum_{i\in R} w_i x_i / \sum_{i\in R} w_i$ with $w_i$ the district's LAEA area. Weights are renormalised over districts with finite values in each month, and the number of valid districts is reported. Drought-affected area is the sum of LAEA district areas in drought classes, expressed in km$^2$ and as a share of the region. Unweighted district means are not used for national or state summaries, so map geometry and area weights share one projection and one boundary vintage.

\subsection*{Era-A reconstruction processing}
The verified Drought Atlas v2 release contains SPEI-1, SPEI-4 and SPEI-12 on a 0.05$^\circ$ grid for January 1901--December 2025\citep{ChuphalData2024}. The grid is split across two disjoint folder trees, both of which are required. Grid cells are intersected with the 724 district polygons in the LAEA projection, and district values are weighted by overlap area, renormalised over valid cells for each month. Six island districts have no Atlas grid cells and remain absent.

The Atlas's own 742-polygon district fabric and 732 district series are used only for quality assurance, because they include disputed or series-less polygons, corrupted names and identifiers that are not a bijection. The published era-A store contains 3,220,872 district-month rows in 375 year partitions. Sampled district-month values were independently recomputed from source cells and matched the stored float32 values exactly. National comparisons with the Atlas's own India series give correlations of 0.9802, 0.9918 and 0.9991 for SPEI-1, SPEI-4 and SPEI-12; the lower variance of district-mean standardized series is expected and is not treated as an identity requirement.

\subsection*{Era-C projection processing}
The projection chain is summarised in Fig.~\ref{fig:erac}. Twenty-four models were inventoried; 22 contained the daily precipitation, maximum-temperature and minimum-temperature fields required for a consistent SPI/SPEI ensemble. CESM2 and IITM-ESM were excluded because daily temperature was unavailable. Model-native calendars are harmonised. Precipitation is conservatively remapped and temperature is bilinearly interpolated to a common 0.25$^\circ$ grid.

Quantile delta mapping is applied per grid cell and calendar month\citep{Cannon2015}. The reference distributions use IMD rainfall and temperature for 1981--2020. Precipitation correction is multiplicative with a zero guard; temperature correction is additive. The full 2015--2100 projection period is used in each model--scenario fit, so extending the record triggers a coherent recomputation rather than concatenation of partial products. Potential evapotranspiration is calculated with Hargreaves--Samani\citep{HargreavesSamani1985} from corrected minimum and maximum temperature, using the same formula for reference and model data. SPI and SPEI are then calculated with the era-B statistical machinery, clipped to $[-5,5]$ with clip counts reported, and aggregated to districts by LAEA area overlap.

Historical-corrected simulations for 1981--2014 are compared with the reference over the same window. Models are ranked with eight metrics: correlation, absolute bias, root-mean-square difference, class agreement, and frequency, duration and severity ratios, plus spatial correlation. The committed subset is selected by the largest inter-rank composite gap and extended to the registered minimum of eight models using stability under 1,000 Dirichlet weight perturbations with seed 20261103. The published subset comprises CanESM5, MPI-ESM1-2-HR, BCC-CSM2-MR, EC-Earth3, ACCESS-ESM1-5, MPI-ESM1-2-LR, IPSL-CM6A-LR and NorESM2-MM.

Ensemble files contain the median, 25th and 75th percentiles, fractions of models at or below $-1.0$ and $-1.5$, model-count fields, per-model member series, and direction-of-change agreement with its exact denominator. Direction agreement compares each model's 2081--2100 mean with its own 1981--2014 historical-corrected mean; it is a model count, not a probability. SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 follow the ScenarioMIP experiments\citep{ONeill2016,Riahi2017}. Projections are not forecasts and are never spliced with era B.

\subsection*{Event, trend and relationship definitions}
Discrete drought events use runs analysis\citep{Yevjevich1967}. The truncation level is $-1.00$ for SPI/SPEI, $-15$\% for SMA and 30\% for VCI. A qualifying event contains at least three consecutive months below the threshold; missing months break runs. Severity is the sum of threshold minus indicator value over event months, and mean intensity is severity divided by duration. Events touching a record boundary are flagged as censored. The frozen event rules are unchanged from version 1.0.0.

Annual trend series require at least nine valid months. Trends use the tie-corrected Mann--Kendall test\citep{Mann1945}, Sen's slope\citep{Sen1968} and the Hamed--Rao autocorrelation correction\citep{HamedRao1998}. District trend maps report Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate results at $q=0.05$\citep{BenjaminiHochberg1995}. Lagged cross-correlations are calculated from $-12$ to $+12$ months with at least 30 paired observations. Confidence intervals use 1,000 moving-block bootstrap resamples with block length $n^{1/3}$ capped between 4 and 24 months and seed 20261103\citep{Kunsch1989}. Correlation and lagged association are not interpreted as causation.

\subsection*{Data engineering and publication}
Era-B values are published as ten wide CSV matrices and as long-format Parquet files with dictionary-encoded identifiers, integer year and month fields, float32 values, zstd compression and deterministic source checksums. Era A is partitioned by variable and year. Era C is partitioned by scenario, statistic, variable and year. The cross-validation product contains matched overlap, per-district and monthly tables. Exact paths are listed in \path{catalog.json}; the browser never discovers files by HTTP globbing.

A monthly era-B refresh runs the extraction, indicator and export stages, incrementally reconverts only changed variables, rebuilds the national aggregate and regenerates the catalogue and status artifact. The regression baseline is reviewed and re-blessed explicitly; the refresh command never approves its own data drift. Versioned data bundles are archived at Zenodo\citep{DeshmukhData2026} and served as an immutable snapshot through static HTTP Range requests. DuckDB-WASM runs analytical SQL in a browser Web Worker, with a permanent CSV fallback for the wide matrices.

\subsection*{Validation design}
Validation is layered rather than single-shot, because each era can only be checked against evidence of a different kind. The pipeline is tested on synthetic worlds with known answers. Era B is benchmarked against an independently processed IMD archive that shares the district fabric, indicator code and reference period but not the input source. Era A is compared with era B over their common 1981--2014 window, which measures historical agreement between a reconstruction and an observation-based gridded product rather than correctness of either. Era C is subjected to committed release gates that test distribution shape, drought statistics, tail behaviour and historical--scenario seam continuity, and the weather-reproduction diagnostics over the short observed overlap are reported but not gated. Every quoted statistic is re-derived by a published-claim harness.

\section*{Technical validation}

\subsection*{Pipeline, fit and round-trip checks}
The era-B pipeline self-test executes 82 deterministic assertions on a synthetic world with an injected drought, vegetation gaps and a missing soil-moisture month. The shipped-data regression harness checks 735 baseline values, including source checksums, catalogue entries, CSV--Parquet round trips, regional anchors, the national aggregate, reality checks and IMD benchmark values. The converter verifies random source cells and absence patterns; the national aggregate was independently recomputed row by row. The Atlas ingestion includes source-cell round trips, and the CMIP6 pipeline includes synthetic end-to-end tests for regridding, bias correction, PET, index calculation, aggregation, ensemble statistics and export.

The indicator fit report records the number of fitted, Gringorten-fallback and calibration-fallback series. Base transformed values are required to remain within the declared mean and standard-deviation gates, and a minimum of 20 base years is enforced. The released \path{data-pipeline/verify_paper_claims.py} script reproduces six methodological experiments, including L-moment parameter recovery, the small-sample maximum-likelihood wet bias, tie handling in the Gringorten transform, classification continuity, area-weighting accuracy and the Benjamini--Hochberg demonstration. In 300 synthetic log-logistic replications at $n=40$, the mean transformed base value was $+0.79$ under maximum likelihood and $+0.01$ under L-moments; this is a property of the tested simulation setting, not a general theorem. On the current SPI-3 record, 415 districts are significant before multiple-testing control and 354 after Benjamini--Hochberg control, superseding the version 1.0.0 values of 382 and 324.

\subsection*{Boundary and documented-event checks}
The served national area is 3,266,386~km$^2$, against India's published 3,287,263~km$^2$, and the decomposition of that shortfall is given above. Three documented-event checks are re-derived from the shipped matrices. Every Rajasthan district is at or below $-1.00$ SPI-3 in July, August and September 2002, with area-weighted means of $-2.55$, $-2.68$ and $-2.54$. All-India SPI-3 is at or below $-1.00$ in 4 of 547 available months: July 1987 and July--September 2002; these form one qualifying three-month event under the frozen rule. For Marathwada in 2015--2016, mean SPI-3 is $-0.97$ in September 2015, mean VCI is 13.5 in November 2015 and 8.4 in January 2016. The checks demonstrate temporal and geographic consistency; they are not an independent accuracy certification. Applying the current default rules to the full SPI-3 matrix yields 8,044 events, of which 61 are right-censored at the record end.

\subsection*{Access without the application}
The dataset can also be used without the application. The wide CSV matrices are convenient for spreadsheet and statistical workflows. Long-format Parquet files support columnar analysis in Python, R or DuckDB, and the catalogue supplies exact paths for HTTP Range reads. The core district-month query pattern is:

\begin{verbatim}
SELECT district_id, year, month, value
FROM read_parquet('data/parquet/observed/variable=spi_03/part-0.parquet')
WHERE district_id = 'rajasthan-jodhpur'
ORDER BY year, month;
\end{verbatim}

A national monthly query uses the pre-aggregate and retains its denominator:

\begin{verbatim}
SELECT year, month, value, n_districts
FROM read_parquet('data/parquet/_national_monthly.parquet')
WHERE variable = 'spi_03'
ORDER BY year, month;
\end{verbatim}

An era-C ensemble query must keep scenario and statistic explicit:

\begin{verbatim}
SELECT district_id, year, month, value, n_models
FROM read_parquet('data/parquet/projections/scenario=ssp245/
                  stat=agreement_moderate/variable=spi_03/year=2090/
                  part-0.parquet')
ORDER BY district_id, month;
\end{verbatim}

Analysts should retain era, source and reference-period fields when combining results, report the valid sample size for every statistic and preserve missing values as missing. Era A, era B and era C should be compared through the published overlap products rather than concatenated. Agreement statistics are model fractions, not probabilities, and direction agreement is a count over a declared window.

\section*{Discussion}

\subsection*{Positioning}
DIE does not compete with operational weekly monitors. It complements them with archive depth, several indicator families at a stable district scale, explicit provenance and a projections layer that is labelled as simulation throughout. The nearest single-product comparison is the Drought Atlas, which supplies a longer reconstruction than any operational product; DIE ingests that reconstruction rather than reproducing it, and adds the gridded multi-source era, the cross-era overlap, the projection ensemble and the query layer. The nearest platform comparison is a general-purpose dashboard; DIE differs by shipping its data contract, its validation artifacts and its figure generator, so that the interface is a view of a citable record rather than the record itself.

\subsection*{What the validation does and does not establish}
The IMD benchmark establishes that era B reproduces an independently processed national observation product at the district scale, with median correlations between 0.60 and 0.71 and drought-state agreement above 82\%. It does not establish that either product is correct at the pixel scale, and the trend comparison shows that the two disagree substantially on the number of significant districts---a difference we report rather than tune away. The overlap comparison establishes that the reconstruction and the gridded record agree to a useful degree where they coexist, with median correlations of 0.72 (SPEI-1) and 0.67 (SPEI-12); it does not identify which is closer to truth, and the 0.78~z median seam standard deviation for SPEI-12 should be read as a disagreement scale, not an error bar. The CMIP6 gates establish that the corrected ensemble has defensible distributional properties, bounded tail and event-statistic breaches, and no seam discontinuity at the historical--scenario transition. They do not establish that the ensemble reproduces the timing of observed events, which is why that diagnostic is reported and not gated.

\subsection*{Limitations}
Several limitations constrain interpretation. District values inherit source-grid and aggregation uncertainty; small districts and buffered island units have reduced effective support. The boundary fabric has a 2011-era lineage and does not represent later reorganisations. CHIRPS, ERA5-Land and MODIS retain their documented biases. Era-B SPEI uses reanalysis PET, whereas era-C SPEI uses Hargreaves--Samani PET; the methods share index construction but not PET physics, so era-B and era-C SPEI are not interchangeable at the margin. VCI's release-dependent baseline can shift when the MODIS record is extended. Event and trend results depend on the declared thresholds and sample rules, and spatial dependence limits the interpretation of district-level significance.

The projection ensemble contains one realisation from each of eight selected models. It does not represent the full CMIP6 structural uncertainty or internal variability, and selection on historical skill does not guarantee future skill. Ensemble spread and agreement must be inspected together. Bias correction preserves the model's relative change signal by construction but cannot remove uncertainties absent from the reference. Correlation, lagged association and propagation displays do not establish causation, and no streamflow, groundwater, crop-loss or health claims should be inferred from these indicators alone. The resource is a research and teaching instrument, not an operational warning service.

\subsection*{Reuse}
Four reuse patterns are supported directly. First, drought climatology: frequency, duration, severity and seasonality can be computed for any district, state, season and indicator without touching the processing chain. Second, evaluation: era C provides a bias-corrected ensemble whose historical segment can be scored against era B and against IMD with the same code. Third, teaching: the guided tour and the labelled eras make the difference between reconstruction, observation-based gridded data and simulation visible in the interface itself. Fourth, methods development: the event, trend and relationship modules are configuration-driven, so alternative truncations, durations and lag windows can be run as sensitivity analyses rather than as new pipelines.
