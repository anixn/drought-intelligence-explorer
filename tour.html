What this is, and how to read it

The Drought Intelligence Explorer is a research platform for Indian drought: one district fabric, three labelled eras, and the same statistical interrogation layer over all of them. Nothing here is operational monitoring, and no era is silently spliced onto another. This page walks you through the four products and their lenses, then shows where every control lives.

Three eras, never one line

Every chart, map and export says which era it shows. A reconstruction, an observation-based gridded product and a climate-model simulation are different kinds of evidence; the dashboard keeps them apart on purpose.

Era A · Reconstruction 1901–2025

The Drought Atlas of India SPEI reconstruction, served at district level. Long historical droughts (1918, 1965, 1972, 1987, 2002) in a record that predates instrumental coverage.

Read it as: a reconstruction, not a measurement.

Era B · Gridded record 1981–present

The multi-source product: SPI and SPEI at 1/3/6/12 months, soil moisture anomaly and VCI, from CHIRPS rainfall, ERA5-Land and MODIS. Each indicator family keeps its own source — they are never blended.

Read it as: observation-based, district-aggregated.

Era C · Projections 2015–2100

8 bias-corrected CMIP6 models (subset of a 22-model archive) under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, shown as ensemble median, spread and model agreement.

Read it as: a simulation of possible futures, never a forecast.

Walk through the products

Ten stops. Each one links straight to the view it describes, with the indicator and month already set where that matters.

Start with one month

The landing view answers a single question — where is drought biting right now — for the month you choose. It opens on July 2002, a documented drought month, so the page is never empty on a first visit. Four numbers lead: drought-affected area, severe plus extreme area, the area-weighted mean indicator, and how many districts are in drought.

Try this: read the receipts row under the numbers — era, product, window, baseline and n. Those four labels are the difference between a number and a claim.

Open the Overview

Choose the indicator and the month

The sidebar is the study context: indicator (SPI / SPEI at four timescales, soil-moisture anomaly, VCI), observation date (year, month, or drag the slider), and the analysis period used by the statistics. On phones the same rail opens as a drawer.

Try this: switch to SPEI-12 and step to September 2002 — the same episode seen through a water-balance lens. Missing months stay missing: they are drawn as gaps and counted, never filled in.

Read the map, then the legend

Districts are coloured with the published McKee severity classes (the same boundaries in every era). The legend carries the exact z-score range for each class; hover or tap a district for its value and class. Areas with no data — including the island districts the Atlas ocean mask omits — are shown as absent, never as “normal”.

Try this: change Colour to the continuous scale to see the gradient inside a class, then switch back. Export the map as a PNG when you need a figure: the caption strip carries the citation, indicator, date and DOI statement.

Open the severity map

Ask whether the record has changed

Historical Analysis answers four questions in order: how often, how long, how severe — and is there a trend? Trends use the Mann–Kendall test with Sen's slope, a 90 % rank-based confidence interval, and the Hamed–Rao correction when serial correlation is present. Annual values need at least nine valid months.

Try this: read the trend panel's p and the autocorrelation note. A trend map without a multiple-testing correction is labelled exploratory; the FDR-corrected count is given for the district set.

Open Historical Analysis

List the events

A drought event is a run of at least three consecutive months below the truncation level (−1.00 z, −15 % soil-moisture anomaly or the 30 % VCI class). Each event reports start, duration, severity (Σ(τ − x)) and recovery, with runs censored at the edge of the record and broken by missing months — never extended across a gap.

Try this: use the sensitivity panel to see how the count changes with duration 4/6/9/12 and truncations −0.50 / −1.50 / −2.00. The default is the published rule, not a preference.

Open Drought Events

Follow the propagation

Drought propagates: rainfall deficits become soil-moisture deficits, which become vegetation stress. The Propagation & Relationships lens shows lagged cross-correlations from −12 to +12 months with at least 30 paired months, and a moving-block bootstrap confidence band (1,000 resamples, block length n^⅓, seed 20261103).

Try this: note what the page refrains from claiming — with rainfall, soil moisture and vegetation only, there is no streamflow or groundwater statement, and correlation is never called causation.

Open Propagation

Look forward, carefully

Projections is era C: pick a scenario, an indicator, a statistic and a time window. “Agreement — moderate” is the default because the honest question is how many models agree that drought gets worse, not what one model says. The median and its 25–75 % band are available beside it.

Try this: compare near-term (2026–2040) with late-century (2081–2100) on the agreement map. The 2015–2025 overlap holds free-running model realisations, not observations — so it is never drawn as a continuation of the record.

Open Projections Drought to 2100 summary

Go back to 1901

The Atlas scrubs the era-A reconstruction one year at a time (only the year you ask for is fetched), for SPEI-1, SPEI-4 and SPEI-12. Class shares for the frame are shown beside the map. Five historical anchors are one click away: 1918, 1965, 1972, 1987 and 2002.

Try this: pick SPEI-12 and step to December 1918. Note that SPEI-4 starts in April and SPEI-12 is a December value — an empty frame explains why, rather than showing placeholder colours.

Open the Atlas

Check the two records against each other

Cross-validation compares era A with era B over the 1981–2014 overlap: correlation, bias, state and class agreement, and the X7 seam uncertainty (the spread of the era B − era A difference). This is the honest bridge between the reconstruction and the record.

Try this: read the bias map first — it is the divergence between the two products, not an error bar for either.

Take it with you, and check it yourself

Every export (CSV, PNG, share card) carries a `#` header with the citation, licence and generation statement, so a file can be traced back to this version. Reading a number honestly

  • Era first. A value without an era label is not interpretable: the reconstruction, the gridded record and the projections are different kinds of evidence.
  • Reference period. Standardised indices are fitted on 1981–2020 (VCI 2000 onwards); a change of baseline changes the z-scores, so the baseline is printed beside the number.
  • Sample size. Every statistic reports n. “718 of 724” and “724 of 724” are different statements, and the six absent island districts are a structural property of the Atlas grid, not a bug.
  • Gaps are data. Missing months are never imputed, interpolated or carried forward; they break runs and are counted in the completeness column.
  • Uncertainty is named. Confidence intervals, the FDR-corrected trend count, the X7 seam spread and the model-agreement share each have a definition in Documentation K.
Not operational. This is a research and communication platform. It does not issue drought declarations, and it does not replace the weekly monitors published by IMD or the India Drought Monitor. For decisions that affect people, use the operational product.
